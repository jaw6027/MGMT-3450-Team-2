# Buyer discovery and outreach drafts

**Drafts only. Verify the recipient and complete Saif's capabilities before use.**

## Initial email

Subject: CNC sourcing for [specific part family] at [company]

Hi [verified name],

I'm [name] with Saif Industries in [verified location]. We machine [verified materials and part types] in [supported quantities].

Does your team use outside CNC suppliers for [specific relevant product/component], prototypes, or production overflow? If so, what supplier requirements would we need to meet to be considered for a paid trial batch?

I can send our capability sheet and review a drawing against your material, inspection and delivery requirements. If another person manages machining suppliers, could you direct me to them?

[Name / business phone / business email]

## Phone opening

“Hello, I'm [name] with Saif Industries. Who handles outside CNC machining suppliers for your team? We would like to understand your requirements before proposing a job.”

## Discovery questions

1. Which parts/processes do you outsource, and which are made in-house?
2. Is there a current CNC sourcing need, a future project, or only a supplier-registration opportunity?
3. What is difficult today: lead times, quality, communication, minimum batches, cost or capacity?
4. What materials, dimensions, critical tolerances and finishes are typical?
5. What are the initial batch size, reorder cadence and forecast uncertainty?
6. What inspection reports, traceability, certifications and insurance are required?
7. Who approves suppliers and who issues purchase orders? Is there a formal application?
8. What delivery windows, shipping destination and payment terms apply?
9. Can you provide a drawing and revision for technical review and quoting?
10. What concrete next step and follow-up timing would be useful?

Record the exact answer, speaker, date and reference. A general vendor list is not proof of CNC outsourcing. A named engineering contact is not automatically a purchasing decision maker.

## Follow-up draft

Hi [name], following up on [actual conversation/date] about [part/project]. We are ready to review [specific information requested]. Is there an appropriate next step or better timing? If this is outside your responsibility, who should handle it?

Use a timing the buyer agrees to or an owner-approved cadence; stop when asked. Do not imply a prior conversation if none occurred.

## After a paid trial

Ask whether dimensions, finish, inspection documentation, communication and delivery met expectations. Record corrective actions before seeking repeat work. Confirm supplier approval explicitly, then discuss reorder quantities, drawing revisions, lead time and whether a blanket PO or scheduled releases would help. Treat forecasts as forecasts until purchase orders are accepted.
