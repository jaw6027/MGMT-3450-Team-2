## Ongoing content mix

Post both company updates and examples of sourcing work. Help buyers understand what Saif can source, why they can trust it, and how to request a quote.

| Content | Starting share | Examples |
|---|---:|---|
| Parts and services | 40% | Inventory highlights, hard-to-find components, sourcing services |
| Customer education | 30% | What to include in an RFQ, part condition and documentation |
| Sourcing success stories | 20% | How the team located a difficult component or handled a requirement |
| Company updates | 10% | Team introductions, milestones, operational improvements |

Use these percentages across a rolling ten-post cycle: four parts/services posts, three educational posts, two sourcing stories, and one company update. The launch schedule introduces the business; the longer-term mix can follow as real, approved sourcing stories become available.

For a sourcing story, explain the customer's need, what Saif did, and the confirmed result. Obtain permission to share identifiable customer or project information. No completed customer case study was supplied, so none has been invented. A useful company update explains a buyer benefit, such as how an inventory-system improvement helps answer availability questions.

## Marketing priorities

**Positioning:** Hard-to-find military and aviation parts. Family-owned since 1999. Over 200,000 unique parts in inventory.

1. **Help buyers find specific parts.** Develop accurate inventory pages using part numbers, descriptions, and available specifications. Consider limited search-ad tests for relevant inventory and sourcing inquiries after the quote process is working.
2. **Make quote requests simple.** Ask for part number, quantity, required condition, documentation requirements, and delivery deadline/destination. Allow parts-list submission if the website supports it. Track actual response times before making a turnaround promise.
3. **Build purchasing confidence.** Explain condition verification and documentation or traceability available for individual items. Use only substantiated certifications and quality claims; a CAGE code is an identifier, not a certification.
4. **Reach relevant procurement teams.** Research aviation maintenance businesses, parts distributors, aerospace contractors, and government purchasing contacts whose requirements match Saif's inventory. Use relevant LinkedIn content and personalized outreach when separately authorized. No messages or supplier applications have been sent.
5. **Use Instagram to show the business.** Share approved inventory highlights, useful explanations, the family business story, and sourcing/fulfillment activity. Direct visitors to the quote process.
6. **Measure business results.** Track qualified RFQs, quote response times, quote-to-order conversion, and repeat purchases. Use engagement and follower counts as supporting indicators rather than proof of sales impact.

AI-powered software can support the service story, but the main buyer message should be finding the right part, receiving reliable information, and getting a prompt response.

| Period | Focus |
|---|---|
| Month 1 | Prepare accurate profiles, inventory/service information, post assets, and a clear RFQ route. Establish baseline inquiry and response-time tracking. |
| Month 2 | Publish consistently and test targeted buyer outreach when authorized. Record inquiry sources and suitable RFQs. |
